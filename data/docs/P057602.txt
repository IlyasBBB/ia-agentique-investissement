Projet : RY URBAN WTR SUPPLY & SANITATION APL
Identifiant : P057602
Pays : Yemen, Republic of (Moyen-Orient, Afrique du Nord, Afghanistan et Pakistan)
Secteur : Eau et assainissement
Coût total : 150.0 millions de dollars
Durée prévue : 101 mois
Année d'approbation : 2002
Statut : termine

Objectif de développement (PDO) :
PDO: To provide efficient and sustainable water and sanitation services in Yemen's major urban areas. APL Program Purpose and Phasing: To provide efficient and sustainable water and sanitation services in Yemen's urban areas through: A. Increase in urban water supplies initially through improved operation and reduction of water losses (Phase I), and subsequently, through development of new water resources including the reuse of wastewater (Phase II). B. Improvement of sector management through establishment of financially viable regional corporations with significant participation of the private sector. C. Provision of affordable sewerage facilities that assure protection of the environment and permit the reuse of wastewater for agriculture or artificial recharge.

Résumé :
The Urban Water Supply and Sanitation Adaptable Program Loan Project provide efficient and sustainable water and sanitation services in the Republic of Yemen's major urban areas. There are three project components to the project, the first component comprises three sub-components which finance civil works, goods, and equipment to rehabilitate and expand the water supply networks and related facilities (pumping stations, reservoirs); to rehabilitate and drill new boreholes; and to rehabilitate and expand wastewater networks and related facilities in eligible urban centers. Funds will also be made available to finance water supply, and water and wastewater projects in six small urban centers with undeveloped water and wastewater networks and that could not qualify to participate in phase one of the project. The second component supports institutional restructuring and improving managerial capacities of local corporations; to put in place an appropriate structure for a regulatory body for the urban water and sanitation sector in Yemen; to finance measures to monitor and audit the operator's performance; prepare studies related to the project; and prepare other water sector projects throughout Yemen. Finally, the third component provides the private operator, who is in a lease contract with Sana'a, with the funds to finance maintenance related works, goods, and consultancy services.

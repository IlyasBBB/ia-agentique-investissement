Projet : Blueing the Caspian Sea: Building Capacities for Pollution Monitoring and Biodiversity Planning
Identifiant : P181526
Pays : Europe and Central Asia (Europe et Asie centrale)
Secteur : Eau et assainissement
Coût total : 8.24 millions de dollars
Durée prévue : 48 mois
Année d'approbation : 2026
Statut : en_cours

Objectif de développement (PDO) :
The Project Development Objective is to strengthen pollution monitoring and biodiversity planning in targeted sites within the Caspian Sea in the Project Focus Countries

Résumé :
The Global Environment Facility (GEF) is providing US$ 11,743,120 in grant financing to the World Bank (WB) and the United Nations Environment Program (UNEP) as GEF Implementing Agencies. The finance will benefit Azerbaijan, Kazakhstan and Turkmenistan. The WB management will seek Board approval for a US$ 8,243,120 project for which United Nations Office for Project Services (UNOPS) will serve as the GEF Executing Agency (Project). A GEF Project Preparation Grant (PPG) of US$183,489 has been awarded to UNOPS for the purposes of preparing the applicable ESF instruments and engagement with the three countries. The UNEP will seek approval per their own procedures for GEF financing and will do internal execution of the grant with the Tehran Convention Interim Secretariat (TCIS). The WB has no fiduciary or supervision accountability for the UNEP-executed project. However, the WB is the implementing agency submitting the combined project to the GEF for CEO Endorsement. This co-implementation arrangement has been agreed between the WB and UNEP. The operation for which the WB receives the GEF grant, will be implemented by UNOPS. It aims to assist countries in managing pollution and improve biodiversity conservation. This will be achieved by strengthening the capacity of the Project Focus Countries (Azerbaijan, Kazakhstan, and Turkmenistan). The Project complements ongoing efforts of countries to meet the obligation of the Tehran Convention and the associated protocols. The Project consists of the following components tailored to support both multi-county and country-specific activities:COMPONENT 1. Strengthening Pollution Monitoring (US$3,150,000). This component will focus on developing systematic approaches and comparable monitoring assessment and action across the three countries in several dimensions of critical impact of pollution on biodiversity, ecosystem health, and water quality. Component activities will focus on common areas at the national level with maximum impact on the Caspian Sea ecosystem and waters including (a) monitoring pollution from freshwater inputs (watersheds and runoffs), (b) enhancing coastal and marine pollution monitoring within the Marine Protected Areas (MPAs) and nature reserves, and (c) addressing pollution originating from oil and gas exploration and production. The component will provide technical assistance (TA) to establish national-level consultative and collaborative mechanisms for private and public sectors in the oil and gas industry with other sectors—notably regarding environment and marine resources—active in the Caspian Sea. The intent is to engage on the application of best practices to monitor and improve control of pollution and explore leveraging of companies’ compliance monitoring of oil and gas industry operations. During Project implementation, each country will convene at one national roundtable, followed by participation in one regional roundtable. The multi-country activities are aimed at benefiting Azerbaijan, Kazakhstan, and Turkmenistan by supporting the development of national pollution monitoring programs aligned with the “Impact Accelerator” approach. These efforts focus on harmonizing policies, sharing knowledge, and enabling access to comparable data through TA, and training,. Key actions involve organizing national and multi-country roundtables to engage a broad range of stakeholders, and developing prefeasibility studies for financial and policy instruments to improve pollution monitoring and reporting, with active contributions and feedback from the respective governments.In Azerbaijan, the Project will assist the Environment Policy Department of the Ministry of Ecology and Natural Resources (AZ MENR) to develop a marine pollution monitoring scheme. Key activities include reviewing current monitoring practices, supporting the development of the monitoring scheme, holding a national workshop for validation, and drafting necessary policy instruments. Capacity building will involve creating a training plan and delivering targeted programs for AZ MENR staff, with a focus on pollutants affecting marine protected areas and river estuaries. The Project will also procure essential monitoring equipment, train staff in its use and maintenance, and help implement outreach and awareness campaigns, culminating in an annual pollution monitoring report in collaboration with national partners.In Kazakhstan, support will be provided to the Kazakh Scientific and Research Institute of the Caspian Sea (KSRICS) under KZ MENR to establish a comprehensive marine pollution monitoring scheme. This will involve a gap analysis, scheme development, stakeholder workshops, and the drafting of policy instruments. The Project emphasizes capacity building through the creation and implementation of a training plan, delivery of targeted training, and integration of best practices into institutional processes. Equipment necessary for effective pollution monitoring, particularly for marine protected areas, will be supplied and staff trained on its use. Awareness activities and preparation of an annual monitoring report in partnership with national organizations are also planned.In Turkmenistan, the CaspEcoControl agency of the Ministry of Environmental Protection (MEP) in Turkmenbashi will be supported to enhance marine pollution monitoring, especially regarding key pollutants from rivers and runoff affecting biodiversity and protected areas. The assistance will include a review of current practices, support for developing a monitoring scheme, stakeholder validation, and policy instrument drafting. The Project will develop and implement a capacity-building plan with targeted training for agency staff, provide vital monitoring equipment, and ensure staff are trained in its operation and maintenance. Additionally, outreach and education campaigns will be conducted, and technical support will be given for the preparation and dissemination of an annual pollution monitoring report involving industry and other partners.COMPONENT 2. Strengthening Biodiversity Planning(US$4,250,000). The component centers on keystone species like Caspian seals and sturgeons to strengthen protected areas management. It addresses challenges in biodiversity data collection by moving from recommendations to actual population reporting. Multi-country activities are designed to support the development of national action programs in Azerbaijan, Kazakhstan, and Turkmenistan, focusing on harmonizing policies, sharing knowledge, and generating comparable pollution monitoring data. The component will deliver TA in areas such as policy development, training, and provision of tools and equipment, leveraging economies of scale through joint guidebooks and training sessions. Key actions include organizing both national and multi-country roundtable discussions to engage diverse stakeholders, presenting findings on policy needs, and collaboratively developing prefeasibility studies for financial and regulatory instruments to advance pollution monitoring and reporting. The participating governments are expected to provide feedback, encourage stakeholder involvement, and consider adoption of the resulting policy recommendations.In Azerbaijan, the component will enhance biodiversity monitoring and planning through the expansion of marine protected areas within Absheron National Park, focusing on flagship species such as Caspian seals, fish, and waterfowl. Key activities include stocktaking of lead species, TA for the development of management plans and policy instruments, stakeholder consultations, and capacity building through training and equipment procurement. The government is expected to provide data, facilitate access to sites, and support stakeholder engagement and outreach.In Kazakhstan, efforts will concentrate on strengthening the Caspian Itbalygy State Nature Reserve in the Mangistau Region, an important habitat for migratory Caspian seals.

Projet : Bangladesh Resilient Urban and Territorial Development Project
Identifiant : P178985
Pays : Bangladesh (Asie du Sud)
Secteur : Gouvernance
Coût total : 600.0 millions de dollars
Durée prévue : 76 mois
Année d'approbation : 2024
Statut : en_cours

Objectif de développement (PDO) :
The project development objective (PDO) is to increase access to resilient urban services and promote coordinated urban and regional development planning and investments in selected economic growth corridors.

Résumé :
The Resilient Urban and Territorial Development Project (RUTDP), approved by the World Bank's Board of Executive Directors on June 21, 2024, effective on January 14, 2025, and scheduled to close on October 31, 2030, provides an important bridge from the current city-based model to an urban cluster/territorial-based approach to urban and regional development through a multi-phased, multi-sectoral investment program to support spatially differentiated, climate resilient and inclusive urban growth in Bangladesh. The project is the first phase of a potential three-phase, Series of Projects (SOP) and targets seven urban clusters and 87 participating urban centers along an approximately 950-kilometer economic corridor extending from Cox's Bazar in the southeast to Panchagarh in the northwest, with an expected direct benefit to approximately 17 million people, including women and vulnerable groups. Since 1999, the World Bank has supported basic urban services development and capacity building of Urban Local Bodies (ULBs) through a series of stand-alone municipal development projects. The project builds on the success of MGSP (P133653), while focusing on strengthening resilient planning and services in secondary cities and improving regional coordination and development along the selected economic corridor and its urban clusters.The project, to achieve the objectives, has investments under three components covering urban infrastructures and services, capacity building and systems improvement, and project management. Prioritized resilient urban infrastructures are focused under Component 1: Climate-Resilient Urban Services and Infrastructure Investments (US$425.93 million, of which US$309.68 million from IDA), delivered through Subcomponent 1.1, which provides subgrants to nodal cities for territorial and regional infrastructure, and Subcomponent 1.2, which supports cluster-wide improvements in basic services in participating Pourashavas and City Corporations, while institutional strengthening, capacity building, Technical Assistance and project implementation for climate resilient and improved urban development are targeted under Component 2 (US$134.07 million, including US$90.32 million from IDA, and including Performance-Based Conditions on operation and maintenance and climate-sensitive urban planning). The third component is for contingent emergency response with a zero-dollar allocation. In June 2026, US$95 million of IDA financing was repurposed from RUTDP to support activation of the Bangladesh Contingent Emergency Response Project (CERP; P517116), enabling the Government to respond promptly to urgent emergency needs. The proposed Additional Financing (AF) addresses this financing gap by restoring the resources repurposed for the emergency response. The infrastructure investments are proposed under Component 1 and include a combination of grey infrastructure such as engineered solutions and embankments with adequate and strategically planned roads, pedestrian walkways and drainage structures, green infrastructure/nature-based solutions to mitigate flooding, heating, and improve living conditions; improve and rehabilitate infrastructure investments to build in resilience; public space enhancement to improve sustainability of amenities; and revenue-generating public facilities (e.g., bus depots, public markets, parks, community centers, truck terminals, abattoirs, and others). This component would also support large regional/cross-cluster/city/township boundary investments along selected corridors. This will include regional services and infrastructure shared by a cluster of cities e.g., regional markets; and small additional investments essential to optimize socio-economic benefits from cluster/corridor-level investments like additional drainage connections and missing culverts, minor access/feeder roads, small bridges, bus and truck terminals, and wholesale markets.

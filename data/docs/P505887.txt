Projet : Burundi: Supporting youth-at-risk through the creative sector
Identifiant : P505887
Pays : Burundi (Afrique de l'Est et australe)
Secteur : Protection sociale
Coût total : 2.75 millions de dollars
Durée prévue : 41 mois
Année d'approbation : 2026
Statut : en_cours

Objectif de développement (PDO) :
The project development objective is to increase economic opportunities in the creative sector for youth at risk in selected areas of the country.

Résumé :
1. Burundi’s demographic profile is marked by a young, rapidly growing population and persistent poor human capital outcomes. With an average fertility rate of six children per woman, annual population growth of 4 percent ranks Burundi ninth globally. Youth account for nearly 20 percent of the population and 35 percent of the workforce. Between 2013 and 2020, about 1.5 million people were ready to enter the labor market, yet this growing workforce faces severe skills gaps. Burundi’s Human Capital Index indicates that a child born today will reach only 39 percent of potential productivity compared to full health and education—an economic loss of roughly 60 percent. Despite free basic education and rising primary enrollment, secondary school transition and completion rates remain low, leaving 11 percent of young Burundians not in employment, education, or training (NEET). Burundi is one of the most exposed countries in the Great Lakes Region to conflict and political tensions. Since gaining independence in 1962, the country has experienced approximately six episodes of conflict , during which many young people engaged in violent actions, driven by the expectation of securing political leadership roles or civil service positions as compensation for their involvement. At the same time, Burundi’s economy—largely dependent on agriculture—faces challenges such as land scarcity and limited land ownership among youth, which remain potential sources of social tension. Integrating young people into community development programs has proven to be an effective strategy for fostering a conflict-resilient society while generating a substantial peace dividend for future generations.2. Although the country has shown commitment to strengthening youth access to economic opportunities, significant challenges persist. Youth NEET, particularly those who did not complete their schooling, are at a heightened risk of exclusion from the labor market and are more likely to perform underpaid jobs. More than 85 percent of the Burundian workforce are still concentrated in rainfed low-productivity subsistence agriculture on increasingly small, fragmented, and degraded plots, vulnerable to the effects of climate change. In 2020, about 3.7 million Burundians were engaged in agriculture, and nearly 79 percent of those employed were cultivating their own land or that of their family without pay. Waged agricultural work represents only six percent of the working population (fewer than 300,000 people). Non-farm employment accounts for just 15 percent of total employment. This unproductive and unpaid agricultural is less attractive to young people, who make up around 60 percent of the working age population.The project development objective is to increase economic opportunities in the creative sector for youth at risk in selected areas of the country. 3. To achieve its development objective, the project will enhance creative skills development and entrepreneurship capacity among selected youth at risk. Beneficiaries will receive a comprehensive package of services designed to promote socio-economic empowerment and resilience. Interventions will include a combination of skills training, coaching, access to finance, provision of start-up toolkits and market linkage support. The project will also provide technical assistance to the Ministry of Youth, Sports, and Culture (MYSC) to promote creative integration into community planning. The project will build the capacity of MYSC to play a stronger role in better supporting implementation of creative initiatives and social cohesion interventions. The total number of direct beneficiaries to be reached is 5,000 youth at risk who will be organized into 200 cooperatives and 50 government officials. The creative business development intervention will target 5,000 beneficiaries (aged 18-35 years-old who are NEET), half of whom will be women. In addition , relevant government officials will receive capacity building support to improve community development planning capacity from a creative economy perspective. Moreover, selected provincial, communal officials and technical focal points managing tourist locations and cultural heritage sites to strengthen their event management skills, marketing and storytelling capacity.The project will be structured around three components :4. Component 1: Creative Skills development and entrepreneurship support for youth at risk. This component will enhance economic opportunities in priority CCI sectors and support business development and formalization. Eligible CCI investments under the project will be identified in each of the four targeted communes based on a comprehensive market assessment. 5,000 Beneficiaries organized into Youth Community Based Organizations(YCBOs) of 20–25 members will be targeted. Approximately 200 cooperatives are expected to be formed and subsequently supported through the formalization process, enabling greater business integration along value chains and facilitating access to capital and markets. Each YCBO will receive: (i) intensive vocational training tailored to creative sectors (e.g., tailoring, crafts, culinary arts, furniture making); digital marketing, and (ii) soft skills development, financial literacy, and coaching over a five-month period. The project will leverage digital learning by providing interactive content accessible via secure digital-enabled devices, providing access to user-friendly digital content, particularly in areas with little or no internet connection. This digital learning would ensure practical, hands-on support that complements theoretical training. Where relevant, vocational training will incorporate green job opportunities, and all eligible investments financed under the project will be screened to mitigate potential adverse climate impacts.Upon completion of the initial learning phase, beneficiaries will receive hands-on support for business plan development, complemented by on-the job-learning opportunities through internship programs. Viable business plans will be financed through the provision of a start-up toolkit of up to US$700 per cooperative which will be encouraged to prioritize the most relevant production tools ensuring complementarities with different business investments from the same location. Continuous technical assistance will be provided throughout an eighteen-month cycle to ensure that cooperatives achieve operational maturity and sustain their investments beyond the duration of project support5. Component 2: Social Cohesion and Institutional Capacity for Creative Sector Development: This component will support trade fairs, exhibitions, and recreational activities, aimed at strengthening social cohesion while preserving culture heritage. 5,000 Youth beneficiaries from Component 1 will receive training and sensitization on social cohesion and will be supported in establishing peer networks to foster collaboration and mutual support. Component 2 will help design and run community activities that support community cultural initiatives at both communal and provincial levels. Eligible activities will include exhibitions and trade fair showcasing products and services developed by cooperatives as well as recreative cultural events. Youth centers and tourism sites within the project area will be leveraged to host these events, creating platforms for peer-to-peer learning, networking, and market linkages among youth beneficiaries. A minimum of 10 exhibitions will be organized throughout the project life cycle. 6. Component 3: Project management, Monitoring and Evaluation & Dissemination. This component will support management, coordination, communication, M&E and learning to ensure effective implementation and monitoring & evaluation for future programming.

Projet : Social Sector Programmatic Development Policy Credit II (SSPC II)
Identifiant : P091365
Pays : Bolivia (Amérique latine et Caraïbes)
Secteur : Éducation
Coût total : 15.0 millions de dollars
Durée prévue : 12 mois
Année d'approbation : 2005
Statut : termine

Objectif de développement (PDO) :
This second phase of the Social Sector Programmatic Development Policy Credit (SSPC) series continues, through a DPC operation, to support Bolivia's efforts to achieve selected MDGs through the implementation of a number of key social sector policies that were designed during the first phase of this program. The SSPC II is expected to support social sector policies with three broad goals in mind: (i) protecting the positive results reached in the last decade in increasing access to and quality of health and nutrition, education, water and sanitation, and social protection; (ii) implementing the sectoral policy frameworks designed under the first phase of this programmatic series to continue progress in social outcomes and move closer to achieving a number of specific Millennium Development Goals (MDGs) by 2015; and (iii) strengthening the capacity of both the Government and civil society to monitor and evaluate policies, increasing accountability and effectiveness. Implementing these policies will advance achievement of specific MDGs by 2015.

Résumé :
This second phase of the Social Sector Programmatic Development Policy Credit (SSPC) series continues, through a Development Policy Credit (DPC) operation, to support Bolivia's efforts in achieving selected Millennium Development Goals (MDGs) through the implementation of a number of key social sector policies designed during the first phase of this program. The SSPC II will support social sector policies, with three broad goals in mind: 1) protecting the positive results reached in the last decade on increasing access to, and quality of health and nutrition, education, water and sanitation, and social protection; 2) implementing the sectoral policy frameworks designed under the first phase of this programmatic series, to continue the progress in social outcomes, and move closer to achieving a number of specific MDGs by 2015; and, 3) strengthening the capacity of both the Government, and civil society to monitor, and evaluate policies, increasing accountability and effectiveness. However, country risks include, on the political side, the changing political situation, and the potential social unrest, as reflected in a steady number of protests; the uncertainty on the outcome of the Hydrocarbons Law; the country's different views on when, and if to call a Constitutional Assembly that would discuss, among other things, regional autonomies; and, the current crisis related to terminating the contract with the company providing water and sanitation services in the populous township of El Alto, adjacent to La Paz. In looking at the economy, GDP growth remains vulnerable to external shocks, notably rising commodity prices and interest rates, while institutional weakness aggravate the situation. To mitigate this, the Government made concerted efforts to address the demands of protesters without resorting to the use of force, and has developed and strengthened new mechanisms for popular political participation. The Bank and the international community have vigorously supported these efforts.

Projet : Pathways to Sustainable Livelihoods Project
Identifiant : P177814
Pays : Lesotho (Afrique de l'Est et australe)
Secteur : Protection sociale
Coût total : 26.5 millions de dollars
Durée prévue : 60 mois
Année d'approbation : 2022
Statut : en_cours

Objectif de développement (PDO) :
To improve the efficiency and equity of selected social assistance programs and to strengthen the livelihoods of poor and vulnerable households

Résumé :
The Project Development Objective is “to improve the efficiency and equity of selected social assistance programs and to strengthen the livelihoods of poor and vulnerable households.” The PDO remains unchanged under the Additional Financing (AF).The AF of US$10 million builds on strong implementation performance under the parent project, which has disbursed 81 percent of financing, achieved Satisfactory ratings on both PDO and Implementation Progress, and exceeded key targets including reaching 9,970 economic inclusion beneficiaries — including 5,873 women — against an end target of 10,000, with 82 percent reporting increased income against a target of 50 percent. The AF presents a catalytic opportunity to deepen these gains while further strengthening the efficiency and equity of Lesotho’s social protection delivery systems. The AF includes a 2.5-year extension of the project closing date to December 28, 2029, to allow for full implementation and consolidation of results, including during the 2027 national elections period.The AF is closely aligned with Lesotho’s National Strategic Development Plan II (2023/24–2027/28), particularly Key Priority Area II on Social Transformation, which prioritizes strengthening social protection, creating jobs as a pathway out of poverty, and investing in youth. It is also aligned with the National Social Protection Strategy II (2021–2031), which promotes protective, promotive, and transformative interventions with a focus on improved delivery systems, resilience, and sustainable livelihoods. The AF operationalizes these frameworks by combining expanded economic inclusion with stronger, more digitally enabled social protection delivery. It is further aligned with the World Bank Group’s Country Partnership Framework for Lesotho FY2024–2028, contributing directly to High-Level Outcome 2 on improved human capital outcomes through stronger social protection systems, and High-Level Outcome 3 on household resilience to climate and other shocks, while advancing the CPF’s cross-cutting priorities on digitalization and gender equality.Component 1 (US$20.3 million total after AF) scales up and deepens the Economic Inclusion Program (EIP) under Subcomponent 1A, extending support to an additional 6,500 poor and vulnerable beneficiaries across all ten districts, increasing total coverage from 10,000 to 16,500. Enhancements to the EIP package include strengthened training on financial and digital literacy, income diversification, climate resilience, and psychosocial support; household sensitization and spouse engagement to address intra-household constraints; referral pathways to childcare and support services; and a pilot of on-demand GBV counseling services co-implemented with the Lesotho Community Resilience and GBV Prevention Project (P515571). The AF also introduces an Intensified Economic Inclusion Program (EIP plus) targeting 150 high-potential young beneficiaries selected from existing EIP participants on the basis of a Growth Readiness Score, providing a second stage of enterprise support including business advisory services, market linkage facilitation, and growth capital of up to US$1,500 per beneficiary. This builds a “ladder of opportunity” for youth, addressing the Government’s declared national emergency on youth unemployment. Implementation will build on lessons from CAFI (P175783) and SADP II (P165228). Subcomponent 1B continues financing assistive devices for persons with disabilities, with no change to allocation.Component 2 (US$8 million, unchanged) retains prepositioned contingency financing to support timely, shock-responsive cash transfer scale-ups, as deployed during the 2023–24 El Niño response. No new allocation is proposed under the AF.Component 3 (US$4.44 million total after AF) consolidates and further strengthens the efficiency, integrity, and fiscal sustainability of social protection delivery systems. The AF will finance the national roll-out of a streamlined, annual OAP Certificate of Existence (CoE) process to address significant fiscal losses from delayed detection of deceased pensioners, and strengthen community death reporting through the NICR. It will also pilot a new mechanism to improve the dynamism of NISSA data, transitioning from infrequent, census-based updates toward a more continuous, responsive data system — tested initially in ten community councils — to strengthen the validity and usability of the social registry across government programs.Component 4 (US$3.75 million total after AF) continues to finance the Project Facilitation Unit (PFU) within the Ministry of Gender, Youth and Social Development (MoGYSD), which retains overall responsibility for coordination, procurement, financial management, environmental and social risk management, monitoring and evaluation, and reporting. An EIP impact evaluation endline survey will be conducted to generate evidence to inform future policy and programming.

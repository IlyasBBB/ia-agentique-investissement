Projet : Water Supply and Sanitation Access Program (PASEA) Project
Identifiant : P178389
Pays : Congo, Democratic Republic of (Afrique de l'Est et australe)
Secteur : Eau et assainissement
Coût total : 400.0 millions de dollars
Durée prévue : 73 mois
Année d'approbation : 2023
Statut : en_cours

Objectif de développement (PDO) :
To increase access to basic drinking water supply and sanitation services in selected provinces of the DRC and to strengthen the capacity of the public and private sector for drinking water supply and sanitation service delivery

Résumé :
The Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC), the largest country in Sub-Saharan Africa and its third most populous, is rich in natural resources but remains one of the world’s poorest countries. With an estimated population of 110 million - over half living in rural areas - and a low population density, poverty is widespread, with 73 percent of households living on less than US$ 1.90 per day. Access to water supply and sanitation (WSS) services is extremely limited: 53 percent of the population lacks basic water access and 84 percent lacks basic sanitation access. Disparities are stark, particularly between urban and rural areas. Despite abundant water resources, the sector is constrained by weak infrastructure, fragmented governance, limited institutional capacity, and insufficient investment, especially in rural and peri-urban areas. The institutional framework remains complex, with overlapping mandates across multiple ministries and agencies, and key sector reforms introduced by the 2015 Water Law - such as the regulatory authority (ARSPE) and the national water resources agency (OCE) are not yet fully operational. Sanitation remains particularly fragmented, with an incomplete legislative framework and unclear responsibilities across the service chain. To address these challenges, the Government of DRC (GoDRC) has developed a national WASH program and a WASH Compact with ambitious targets and financing needs of US$ 20 billion. The Water Supply and Sanitation Access Program (PASEA) adopts a long-term Multi-Phase Approach (MPA) over 11 years, with a projected IDA financing envelope of US$1.25 billion. The program aims to expand access to sustainable WSS services while strengthening institutional capacity and enabling private sector participation. Phase 1 (US$ 350 million) focuses on the provinces of Kasai, Kasai Central, Kasai Oriental, and Kwilu, targeting the most underserved areas based on access gaps, poverty, and development priorities. It aims to provide basic water services to 2 million people and sanitation services to 1.2 million people, using an area-based approach at decentralized territorial levels. Subsequent phases will progressively scale up geographically, with increasing provincial responsibility and potential co-financing. PASEA is aligned with the World Bank’s Country Partnership Framework (2022–2026), contributing to improved access to infrastructure, stronger governance, and private sector development. By prioritizing fragile and conflict-affected regions, the program also seeks to strengthen the social contract and support inclusive economic development. Overall, the project combines infrastructure investments with institutional strengthening and private sector development to sustainably expand WASH services and prepare for scale-up in subsequent phases.Component 1: Increasing access to and capacities for drinking water supply service delivery (IDA: US$ 254.3 million). This component supports sector reforms, strengthens governance and institutional capacity at national and provincial levels, and expands water supply infrastructure in rural and peri-urban areas. It also promotes private and not-for-profit operator development and builds a skilled workforce for solar-powered systems. It includes the rehabilitation of the Kananga water treatment plant, transferred from the Access, Governance and Reform for the Electricity and Water Sectors Project (AGREE Project). Subcomponent 1.1: Expanding access to water supply (IDA: US$ 230.9 million) finances the construction, rehabilitation, and expansion of water systems (boreholes, networks, water treatment plant, surface water intake, storage, tap stands, connections, metering), including solar-powered systems with backup. It also covers studies, supervision, safeguards, and support to ONHR and not-for-profit operators (ASUREPs). Subcomponent 1.2: Strengthening private and not-for-profit operators (IDA: US$ 6.6 million) supports PPP structuring, service delegation contracts, tariff analysis, operator capacity building, and result-based financing to expand services. Subcomponent 1.3: Strengthening public sector capacity (IDA: US$ 16.8 million) supports policy development, institutional strengthening, and operationalization of provincial utilities. It includes technical assistance, equipment, performance systems, communication campaigns, and training programs.Component 2: Increasing access to and capacities for sanitation service delivery (IDA: US$ 53.7 million). This component strengthens the sanitation policy and institutional framework while scaling up access to basic sanitation and improving WASH in schools and health centers. It also promotes behavior change, private sector development, and workforce skills. Subcomponent 2.1: Expanding sanitation and hygiene access (IDA: US$ 25.7 million) supports implementation of the open defecation eradication roadmap using Community-Led Total Sanitation (CLTS) approaches, combined with material support in rural areas and sanitation marketing and e-vouchers in peri-urban areas. Subcomponent 2.2: Improving WASH in institutions (IDA: US$ 20..0 million) finances construction and rehabilitation of WASH facilities in schools and health centers (325 in total), strengthens O&M systems, and supports hygiene promotion, including menstrual hygiene management. Subcomponent 2.3: Strengthening the private sector for sanitation (IDA: US$ 3.1 million) supports sanitation enterprises through capacity building, market development, and catalytic grants for products and services. Subcomponent 2.4: Strengthening public sector capacity (IDA: US$ 4.9 million) supports development of the legal and regulatory framework, urban sanitation planning, institutional strengthening, MIS development, and training programs, with a focus on scaling up future investments.Component 3: Program Management, Learning and Scale-up (IDA: US$ 42 million). Sub-component 3.1: Project management and learning (IDA: US$ 36.0 million) finances overall implementation, coordination, and monitoring of MPA-Phase 1. It supports the operation of the Project Coordination Unit (PCU), establishment and functioning of Provincial Project Implementation Units (PPIU) in Kasai, Kasai Central, Kasai Oriental, and Kwilu, and capacity building of national and provincial entities. It also covers project M&E systems, grievance redress and GBV-related measures, learning and knowledge exchanges, and a Young Professionals program to strengthen fiduciary, technical, environmental, and social capacities. In addition, it finances independent verification (IVA), audits, and land acquisition and resettlement costs. Sub-component 3.2: Preparing scale-up for MPA-Phase 2 (IDA: US$ 6.0 million) supports preparation of the next phase in additional provinces (including Lomami and Kongo Central, with potential expansion). It finances preparatory studies (baseline, feasibility, technical, environmental, and social), project design, and initial operational costs for future project implementation units, including staffing and equipment.Component 4: Contingency Emergency Response Component (US$ 0 allocated).

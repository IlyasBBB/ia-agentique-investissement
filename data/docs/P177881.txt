Projet : Ethiopia Education and Skills for Employability Project
Identifiant : P177881
Pays : Ethiopia (Afrique de l'Est et australe)
Secteur : Éducation
Coût total : 200.0 millions de dollars
Durée prévue : 63 mois
Année d'approbation : 2023
Statut : en_cours

Objectif de développement (PDO) :
The Project Development Objective is to improve employment outcomes of the TVET system of Ethiopia with a focus on women and marginalized groups.

Résumé :
The Ethiopia Education and Skills for Employability (EASE) Project aims to improve employment outcomes of Ethiopia’s technical and vocational education and training (TVET) system, with a focus on women and marginalized groups. The parent project, approved on September 29, 2023, supports youth in acquiring market-relevant skills while strengthening the skills development system through better alignment of training provision with labor market demand. The proposed Additional Financing (AF) will extend the project’s reach to refugee and host community youth, expanding their access to market-relevant skills development and pathways to employment and self-employment while strengthening national systems to support sustainable economic inclusion.The AF responds to Ethiopia’s evolving agenda for refugee inclusion and skills development. The Government’s 2026–2030 Makatet (Inclusion) Roadmap provides a national framework for strengthening refugee self-reliance and socioeconomic inclusion, including through expanded economic opportunities and integration into national systems. The AF is also closely aligned with the National Technical and Vocational Training Strategy, launched in November 2025, which emphasizes demand-driven and competency-based training, stronger private sector engagement, improved youth employability, and better outcomes for women and marginalized groups.Building on the parent EASE Project, the AF will strengthen both skills development and pathways to jobs. Component 1 expands access to market-relevant TVET programs and short-term skills training, including entrepreneurship, digital, and green skills, while strengthening links to wage employment and self-employment opportunities. Public polytechnic colleges will be supported to improve the relevance and quality of training through strategic investments, updated curricula, industry advisory boards, and industry-led training. Under Component 2, flexible, market-responsive short-term training is delivered through public and private providers, including through performance-based approaches that link provider incentives to job placement and retention outcomes. Component 3 strengthens the broader skills development ecosystem through investments in a skills management information system, stronger public-private coordination, and enhanced Government capacity to implement inclusive and sustainable reforms. It will also advance the recognition of prior learning and portable micro-credentials, allowing existing skills and competencies to be assessed, certified, and linked to further training and employment opportunities. Together, these interventions aim to strengthen pathways from skills development to productive employment and improved livelihoods for refugee and host community youth, while reinforcing the national systems needed to sustain their economic inclusion beyond the life of the project.

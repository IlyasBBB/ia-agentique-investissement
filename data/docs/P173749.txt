Projet : Benin Electricity Access Scale-up (BEAS) Project
Identifiant : P173749
Pays : Benin (Afrique de l'Ouest et centrale)
Secteur : Énergie
Coût total : 200.0 millions de dollars
Durée prévue : 87 mois
Année d'approbation : 2021
Statut : en_cours

Objectif de développement (PDO) :
The PDO is to increase access to electricity services for households, enterprises, and public facilities.

Résumé :
Summary of Proposed Restructuring. The proposed Level 2 restructuring of the Benin Electricity Access Scale-up Project (BEAS, P173749) would not modify the Project Development Objective (PDO), which remains to increase access to electricity services for households, enterprises, and public facilities. The restructuring would introduce three main changes: (i) a 20-month extension of the project closing date, from December 31, 2026 to August 31, 2028, to allow completion of ongoing distribution grid construction works; (ii) a reallocation of uncommitted resources from Component 2, Policy and Regulatory Actions anchored in Performance-Based Conditions, to Components 1 and 3 to partially address cost overruns under distribution network works and strengthen technical assistance and implementation support; and (iii) revisions to selected intermediate results indicators to reflect actual unit costs established through competitive procurement.Revised Component Allocations and Disbursement Status. The total project cost would remain unchanged at US$200 million. The restructuring would revise component allocations as follows: Component 1, On-grid Electrification, would increase from US$183 million to US$187 million to partially cover cost overruns under distribution grid construction; Component 2, Policy and Regulatory Actions anchored in Performance-Based Conditions, would be reduced from US$7 million to US$4,000, reflecting the near-total transfer of uncommitted resources given its near-zero financial execution despite substantial physical progress; and Component 3, Technical Assistance and Implementation Support, would increase from US$10 million to US$12.996 million to finance technical assistance activities, including the GIS platform, and remaining implementation support needs. As of the restructuring date, US$107.25 million had been disbursed equivalent to approximately 55.6 percent, leaving an undisbursed balance of US$85.50 million.Rationale for Extension of the Closing Date. The proposed closing date extension is necessary to enable completion of four of the six awarded distribution network construction contracts, whose implementation schedules extend beyond the current closing date. Implementation delays are mainly attributable to the absence, at effectiveness, of feasibility studies, Environmental and Social Impact Assessments, and Resettlement Action Plans. These preparatory studies and instruments were completed more than three years after effectiveness, which substantially delayed commencement of grid extension and densification works. Two contracts are expected to be completed within the original implementation period; however, the remaining four contracts require an additional 20 months to be completed.Without the proposed extension, the project would complete only the first two contracts, corresponding to approximately 21.4 percent of the distribution line target and 36.6 percent of the transformer capacity target. With the requested 20-month extension, the project is expected to construct approximately 3,097 km of low- and medium-voltage distribution lines, representing 55.3 percent of the original 5,600 km target, and install 90,690 kVA of transformer capacity, representing approximately 113 percent of the original target. The increase in transformer capacity reflects needs identified during field-based feasibility studies.Cost Escalation and Results Framework Adjustments. The competitive bidding process confirmed significant cost increases, estimated at approximately 40 percent, driven largely by the effects of the COVID-19 pandemic and the war in Ukraine on supply chains and construction costs. As a result, the original project budget is insufficient to achieve certain intermediate infrastructure targets, particularly those related to low- and medium-voltage distribution lines under the grid extension and densification works. Accordingly, selected intermediate results indicators would be revised to reflect the actual unit costs established through competitive bidding and the outputs that can be delivered within the available financing envelope.Implementation Performance. Project implementation performance remains satisfactory. The Project Implementation Unit has demonstrated strong performance across key dimensions of project management, and the Task Team considers that the remaining activities can be completed within the proposed extension period, provided that implementation momentum is maintained.Adjustments to Component 2 Activities. In addition to the proposed reallocation of funds, three of the eight activities originally included under Component 2, together with their associated indicators, would be transferred to Component 3. These activities have not yet been completed. The transfer is intended to facilitate implementation by financing them through Component 3, which is not subject to the Performance-Based Conditions framework. This adjustment responds to implementation constraints related to the limited documentation available to support expenditures under completed Component 2 activities and the Borrower’s concern that such expenditures could be deemed ineligible. The proposed transfer would therefore help ensure completion of the remaining activities and achievement of the relevant targets.Key Achievements to Date. The project has made substantial progress toward achievement of the PDO. To date, 762,606 people have been provided with new or improved electricity services, against an end target of 780,000, including 388,929 women. A total of 146,655 households have been connected to the grid, representing 97.8 percent of the target of 150,000. In addition, 19,803 LED streetlights have been installed, representing 99 percent of the target of 20,000; 2,789 enterprises have been connected, representing 279 percent of the target of 1,000; and 556 public facilities have been connected, representing 111 percent of the target of 500. Under Component 2, the adoption of a low-connection-charges policy has resulted in 254,454 new households being incorporated into SBEE’s commercial database, and the Gender Quota Policy has been adopted.Revised Results Framework and Disbursement Deadline. The revised Results Framework would update selected intermediate infrastructure indicators to reflect the outcomes of the competitive bidding process and the transfer to Component 3 of three intermediate indicators that have not yet been achieved under Component 2. The proposed revised closing date is August 31, 2028, and the deadline for withdrawal applications would be December 31, 2028.

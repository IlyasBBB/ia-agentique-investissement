Projet : Development Response to Displacement Impacts Project in the Horn of Africa Phase II
Identifiant : P178047
Pays : Ethiopia (Afrique de l'Est et australe)
Secteur : Protection sociale
Coût total : 180.0 millions de dollars
Durée prévue : 84 mois
Année d'approbation : 2022
Statut : en_cours

Objectif de développement (PDO) :
To improve access to basic social and economic services, expand livelihood opportunities and enhance environmental management for host communities and refugees in the target areas.

Résumé :
The Project Development Objective is "to improve access to basic social and economic services, expand livelihood opportunities and enhance environmental management for host communities and refugees in the target areas." The project will run for six and half years (2022-2029), with a total budget of US$280 million, which will be provided on full grant terms. The project is implemented by the Ministry of Agriculture, which maintains a Project Coordination Unit at the federal, regional and woreda (district) levels. Close collaboration is maintained with the Government of Ethiopia's Refugees and Returnees Services (RRS) and the United Nations High Commissioner for Refugees (UNHCR). Geographic coverage. DRDIP II operates in the six main refugee-hosting regions (Afar, Amhara, Benishangul-Gumuz, Gambella, Somali and Tigray), covering 29 woredas and 382 kebeles (villages). Component One: Social and economic services and infrastructure (US$117.6 million): this component finances interventions to improve access to basic social services and economic infrastructure while building institutional capacities for local development planning and implementation. Support will also be provided to enhance women's leadership and voice and social cohesion and inclusion between refugees and host communities. There are five sub-components (a): Community Investment Fund (CIF) & Strategic Investment Fund (SIF); (b): Capacity-building for local planning & decentralized service delivery; (c) Women's leadership and voice; (d): Social cohesion and inclusion; and a newly introduced sub-component 1(e) under the 2026 Additional Financing, being the Displacement Contingency Response Mechanism, which is a US$5 million contingency fund to provide rapid support to districts that experience large-scale refugee inflows. Component Two: Sustainable Environmental Management (US$23.9 million): this component addresses environmental degradation, which is exacerbated by the large refugee presence and climate change. The two sub-components are: (a) Integrated natural resource management; and (b) Promote Efficient and Alternative Energy Sources. Component Three: Sustainable Livelihood Program (US$113.5 million): activities to improve livelihood and income-generation are implemented through four sub-components: (a) Support to traditional (on-farm and off-farm) livelihoods; (b) Support to non-traditional (non-farm) livelihoods and business development; (c) Small Scale, Micro and Household Irrigation Schemes; and (d) Local Economic Development (LED), which was introduced through the 2026 Additional Financing. Component Four: Project Management, Monitoring and Evaluation and Learning (US$25 million): this component supports the effective management of the project and systems to monitor progress and evaluate outcomes. It also supports a project communications strategy. Component Five: Contingent Emergency Response Component (CERC) (US$0 million). In the event of an eligible natural hazard or emergency crisis, funds may be reallocated from other components. This component, if activated, would finance rapid response measures and early recovery activities to address disaster, emergency and/or catastrophic events.

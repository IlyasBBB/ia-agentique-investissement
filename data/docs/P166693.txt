Projet : Labor Market Information and Skills System Transformation for Labor Market Flexibility
Identifiant : P166693
Pays : Indonesia (Asie de l'Est et Pacifique)
Secteur : Protection sociale
Coût total : 154.0 millions de dollars
Durée prévue : 47 mois
Année d'approbation : 2024
Statut : en_cours

Objectif de développement (PDO) :
To improve access to and effectiveness of skills development and employment services for the working-age population

Résumé :
Indonesia’s economic growth and employment are recovering from the shock of the COVID-19 pandemic, but the progress is unequal. The 5 percent real GDP growth rate between 2015 and 2019 was interrupted in 2020 following the onset of the global COVID-19 pandemic. Notwithstanding, the economy showed signs of resilience: real GDP growth accelerated from 3.7 percent in 2021 to 5.4 percent year-on-year in the first three quarters of 2022. Unemployment rates fell from 7.1 percent in August 2020 to 5.9 percent in August 2022. At the same time, several factors are undermining the contribution of jobs to growth and well-being – including low labor productivity and increasing job polarization, which can lead to higher inequality if not managed well. Globalization, greening, and technology have changed the location, content, and structure of work, resulting in rapid shifts in demand for occupations and skills. A business-as-usual trajectory will not make it possible to leverage the new growth opportunities emerging from Indonesia’s reform efforts. Low quality (granularity) of available labor market information and lack of integration limits the responsiveness of the workforce development system to the needs of students, job seekers, workers, practitioners, and employers. This further exacerbates the problem of weak labor market relevance of technical and vocational training due to low quality, lack of partnerships between institutes and industries, and partial implementation of competency-based training. In 2022, the GoI issued Presidential Decree 68 on revising the Technical and Vocational Education and Training (TVET) System. Presidential Decree 68 recognizes the leadership of the Ministry of Manpower (MoM) in setting strategic direction for vocational training, with a strong emphasis on ensuring the system's responsiveness to upskilling and reskilling needs of the workforce that is not in formal education. A more robust labor market information system (LMIS) with up-to-date information is a prerequisite for the MoM to provide the needed strategic direction to the system. Within this context, this project aims to provide a catalytic and enabling environment for the implementation of planned reforms in the face of the changing labor market demand by addressing the following challenges: (i) quality, accessibility, and utilization of information on labor market demands and skills to increase the efficiency of the labor market; (ii) integration of skill training and job matching services to increase employability post-training; (iii) quality and relevance of training through enhanced linkages with the private sector and improved governance of TVET system. The proposed operation is strategically relevant to the Government’s objective of strengthening vocational education and training for Industry 4.0 as identified in the RPJMN (2020-2024). Its development objective to improve access to and quality skills development and employment services for the working-age population also aligns clearly with the World Bank Group’s Indonesia Country Partnership Strategy (CPF) FY21-25. To achieve its objective, the Project proposes the following three components. Component 1 (Fostering a reliable Labor Market Information System) aims to improve the quality, relevance, and accessibility of labor market information services for students, job seekers, workers, employers, and policymakers through a combination of necessary ICT system improvements, capacity building for data collection and analysis and improved service delivery (better access and improved processes, centered on the client need). Component 2 (Leveraging labor market information to promote a higher quality, integrated and green training ecosystem) aims to establish an equitable, regionally competitive, and climate-friendly skill pool nationwide by establishing up to 22 new CMTCs (Centrally Managed Training Centers), in strategic areas with future potentials for regional competitiveness, and on upgrading the training quality of existing 21 CMTCs nationwide. Finally, component 3 will focus on Institutional capacity and project management. The project’s coverage is nationwide, given its focus on system-level reforms.

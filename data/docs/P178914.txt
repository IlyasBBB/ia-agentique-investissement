Projet : Emergency Power Restoration Project
Identifiant : P178914
Pays : Malawi (Afrique de l'Est et australe)
Secteur : Énergie
Coût total : 60.0 millions de dollars
Durée prévue : 65 mois
Année d'approbation : 2022
Statut : en_cours

Objectif de développement (PDO) :
To rehabilitate and increase resilience of the Kapichira hydropower dam and spillways, and transmission and distribution infrastructure damaged by Tropical Storm Ana.

Résumé :
Despite ambitions to improve and grow Malawi’s power sector, the sector continues to be vulnerable to climate change. On January 24, 2022, Tropical Storm Ana made landfall in southern Malawi. Due to the storm’s landfall, heavy rains exceeding 150 mm (up to 300 mm in some areas) in a day and very strong winds exceeding 80 km/hour caused flash flooding that affected people, property and infrastructure. The storm caused 46 fatalities, affected over 221,000 households, and displaced more than 190,000 people. In March 2023, Cyclone Freddy caused additional damage to infrastructure in southern Malawi.The electricity sector was one of the worst affected sectors, with damage to the 129 MW Kapichira Hydropower Plant rendering the plant out of commission. Transmission and distribution infrastructure in Southern Malawi was affected, with 14 transmission lines and several distribution lines requiring emergency restoration. Damage was also incurred to infrastructure associated with the Shire Valley Transformation Programme (SVTP), including the irrigation intake located adjacent to the damaged section of Kapichira Dam. The SVTP is Malawi’s flagship irrigation programme aimed at developing approximately 43,000 hectares of irrigated agriculture in the lower Shire Valley. The permanent intake for the scheme is integrated into Kapichira Dam, making completion of the dam rehabilitation essential for realization of the full irrigation benefits of SVTP.In response to the Government’s request for support to the energy sector, IDA Crisis Response Window (CRW) financing in the amount of US$60 million was approved, on an urgent basis, to restore damaged electricity generation, transmission and distribution infrastructure. The Project, scheduled to end in November 2026, was designed in two phases: (i) Phase 1 focused on emergency restoration of power generation and transmission functionality; and (ii) Phase 2 focused on permanent rehabilitation and climate-resilient reconstruction of damaged infrastructure. Phase 1 was successfully completed by 2023 and restored generation at Kapichira and transmission capability through temporary emergency measures, bringing approximately one-third of Malawi’s electricity supply back online. The temporary solutions remain functional but were designed as interim measures and continue to expose the power system to elevated operational and dam-safety risks. However, detailed geotechnical investigations, hydrological studies, hydraulic modelling and engineering design undertaken during implementation demonstrated that the permanent rehabilitation requirements for Kapichira Dam are substantially larger, more complex and more time-consuming than originally anticipated. As a result, it is no longer feasible to complete the full permanent rehabilitation works within the anticipated timeframe and financing envelope of MEPRP. In light of the increased scope, cost and implementation period required for permanent rehabilitation of Kapichira, the original component allocations and implementation approach under MEPRP require adjustment. The proposed restructuring, with the Government of Malawi's agreement, therefore preserves the emergency objectives of the Project by maintaining dam safety and operational continuity at Kapichira while transitioning responsibility for completing the permanent rehabilitation works to the proposed MPA. At the same time, resources are reallocated from Component 1 to the more advanced Component 2 to finance critical transmission and distribution investments that can be substantially completed within the revised closing period of MEPRP as well as make best use of the CRW funds.Component 1: Kapichira Hydropower Dam Rehabilitation and Strengthening (restructured allocation of approximately US$27.6 million, led by EGENCO). Under the restructuring, this component will focus on maintaining safe operation of Kapichira under the current state of emergency through dam safety oversight, operation of the Owner’s Engineer and Independent Panel of Experts (IPOE), emergency preparedness measures, procurement and mobilization of the rehabilitation contractor, and advance payment for the permanent rehabilitation works once contracts are signed. The permanent rehabilitation and climate-proofing of Kapichira Dam, including the embankment dam, spillways, sediment management measures and integrated permanent SVTP intake, will be financed and completed under the first phase of the proposed Multi-Phase Programmatic Approach (MPA). The restructured component therefore focuses on preserving dam safety, maintaining emergency preparedness, and ensuring readiness for a seamless transition to the new operation.Component 2: Transmission and Distribution Network Restoration (restructured allocation of approximately US$32.4 million, led by ESCOM). This component will finance completion of the permanent rehabilitation of transmission and distribution infrastructure damaged by Tropical Storms Ana and Freddy, together with additional urgent investments required to safeguard reliable operation of Malawi’s transmission and distribution network. Activities include rehabilitation of damaged lines and substations, emergency preparedness measures, climate resilience investments, operational safeguarding equipment, and completion of critical grid infrastructure, including equipment associated with the Phombeya substation such as the Variable Shunt Reactor that remained unfunded at completion of the Mozambique–Malawi Interconnector Project. These investments are required to maintain voltage stability, strengthen system reliability, and support the secure integration of generation from Kapichira, regional interconnections, and future renewable energy investments. The contract for the major works under this component is under implementation.

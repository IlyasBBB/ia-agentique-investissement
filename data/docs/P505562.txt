Projet : Yemen Improved Water Management and Irrigation for Sustainability and Efficiency Project
Identifiant : P505562
Pays : Yemen, Republic of (Moyen-Orient, Afrique du Nord, Afghanistan et Pakistan)
Secteur : Eau et assainissement
Coût total : 153.65 millions de dollars
Durée prévue : 73 mois
Année d'approbation : 2026
Statut : en_cours

Objectif de développement (PDO) :
The Project Development Objective is to strengthen the climate and water information management system, and enhance climate-resilient irrigation services and access to water supply and sanitation services in selected basins.

Résumé :
This Project is the first phase in the Multiphase Programmatic Approach (MPA) and focuses on improving climate and water information management, enhancing water security, irrigation services, and water supply and sanitation in two basins, Aden-Tuban and Wadi Hajir. The Project includes four components designed around a robust systems approach that responds to the complex, FCV context in Yemen. These are informed by deep analysis and contextual awareness of the key issues in the water and agricultural sectors and include a high degree of flexibility built around a combination of top-down and bottom-up interventions. These components are 1: Climate and Water Information Management Systems (CWIMS), 2: Improved Integrated Watershed Management and Irrigation, 3: Resilient Water Supply & Sanitation Systems and WASH Services in selected areas and 4: Project Support, Management, Evaluation and Administration. The Project will be implemented through UN agencies that exhibit technical understanding and readiness for implementation in specified basins. Financial Management Framework Agreement (FMFA) and Alternative Procurement Plan (APA), allowing UN agencies to use their own Financial Regulations and procurement procedures as APA under the Procurement Framework Policy Section III.F.Under the Project, UNDP and UNOPS will serve as grant recipients and implementing entities. These agencies have robust and adaptable implementation arrangements tailored to local needs and will strengthen the capacity of local partners, whose past commitment demonstrates strong potential. The two UN agencies will provide technical assistance and training to local institutions, progressively transferring implementation technical capacities to them while retaining overall fiduciary responsibility and accountability. Local partners, high-performing private contractors, and community groups will be engaged for implementation. The Third-Party Monitoring Agents (TPMAs) will be engaged in monitoring and verification of all Project interventions, conducting quarterly supervision visits to Project sites for technical, safeguards, and fiduciary verifications.

Projet : Skills Training for Results and Industry-Valued Employment Project
Identifiant : P514247
Pays : Zambia (Afrique de l'Est et australe)
Secteur : Éducation
Coût total : 80.0 millions de dollars
Durée prévue : 60 mois
Année d'approbation : 2026
Statut : en_cours

Objectif de développement (PDO) :
The objective of the project is to strengthen the skills ecosystem for increased job creation in priority value chains.

Résumé :
The Skills Training for Results and Industry-Valued Employment (STRIVE) project is an US$80 million IDA grant to the Republic of Zambia, designed as Phase 5 of the Skills for Economic Transformation and Jobs (SET4Jobs) Multiphase Programmatic Approach in Eastern and Southern Africa. The project development objective is to strengthen the skills ecosystem for increased job creation in priority value chains. STRIVE targets three sectors with strong private investment and job creation potential (mining, agribusiness, and hospitality) through an integrated three-component design. Component 1 (US$10 million) builds industry-led skills systems by developing information infrastructure (a TEVET Management Information System and a Labor Market Information System with demand forecasting), strengthening sector coordination and occupational standards, and reforming the TEVET Fund's operational guidelines through a Performance-Based Condition that links the system's own financing to verified employment outcomes. Component 2 (US$60 million) prepares youth for better jobs through competitive institutional grants for advanced programs and Centers of Excellence (Window A), quality improvement grants for crafts and trades institutions (Window B), a student loan pilot for TEVET students through the Higher Education Loans and Scholarships Board, and results-based contracts that shift short-term training for 150,000 beneficiaries from input-based to outcome-based financing. Component 3 (US$10 million) grows employment opportunities by strengthening firm capabilities through performance-based business development services for 350 growth-oriented SMEs and cluster-based upskilling through business associations in priority value chains. Over the five-year implementation period, the project is expected to reach approximately 350,000 beneficiaries (238,000 direct and 112,000 system-level), with at least 40 percent women, and is implemented by the Ministry of Technology and Science through a coordination model that channels investments through existing institutional systems rather than creating parallel structures.

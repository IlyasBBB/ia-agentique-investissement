Projet : Housing Finance, Lands and Sustainable Investments
Identifiant : P513352
Pays : Kenya (Afrique de l'Est et australe)
Secteur : Finance
Coût total : 910.0 millions de dollars
Durée prévue : 63 mois
Année d'approbation : 2026
Statut : en_cours

Objectif de développement (PDO) :
The objectives of the Project are to expand access to affordable housing finance to underserved beneficiaries, improve efficiency in property registration, and support Kenya’s commitments on environment and energy access, effectively contributing to job creation.

Résumé :
The earlier Kenya Affordable Housing Finance Project (KAHFP) (P165034) made significant strides in expanding access to affordable housing finance in Kenya, primarily through the support and operationalization of the Kenya Mortgage Refinance Company (KMRC). As of December 2025, KMRC refinanced approximately KES 25.3 billion in mortgages, representing 5,148 loans. Repayment tenor lengthened from 8.3 (2021) to 12.3 years (2025) signaling enhanced affordability. Notably, nearly 48 percent of KMRC-refinanced mortgages were issued to women, highlighting significant progress in gender inclusion. KMRC’s portfolio now accounts for about 5.0 percent of the banking sector’s mortgage book by value and 12.8 percent by number of loans. Additionally, the share of refinancing through SACCOs within KMRC’s portfolio grew from 7 percent in 2022 to 15.2 percent by December 2025, indicating a strengthened role for non-bank lenders in the affordable housing finance sector and growing financial inclusion. These achievements demonstrate that KMRC is actively driving the market forward in a positive direction; however, affordability remains a binding constraint for the majority. The Housing Finance, Lands and Sustainable Investments Project - is aimed at strengthening the Kenyan housing market by addressing some of the lingering demand and supply gaps in the sector. To ensure continuity, it proposes to support KMRC with a line of credit for mortgage refinancing, blended with KMRC’s own funding raised through bond issuances on the capital markets, to ensure leverage of the IDA/IBRD resources and the overall sustainability of the mortgage market. The project will aim to deliver additionality by moving beyond conventional mortgage financing and exploring more innovative products that can reach underserved segments of the market. It will also support the State Department of Lands and Physical Planning (SDLPP) in its reforms to make property registration faster, cheaper and more reliable, which is a precondition to develop the mortgage market. Additionally, the project proposes to support the Government of Kenya (GoK), via the National Treasury (NT), in launching a sovereign sustainability-linked loan (SLL). The innovative instrument ties the cost of borrowing to national sustainability targets, aligning Kenya’s financing needs with measurable environmental and development outcomes that would also have co-benefits for housing. It will also support a sub-sovereign sustainability bond issuance by KMRC.The four components of the project include: Component 1: Support to KMRC. This component will provide a line of credit to KMRC and ensure continuity and enhanced uptake of the company’s current product offering while innovating to serve unmet market needs. IBRD will provide a credit line to KMRC through the NT to refinance affordable housing loans. As in the first phase of the project, KMRC will extend loans to banks and Savings and Credit Cooperative Organizations (SACCOs) collectively known as Primary Mortgage Lenders (PMLs) that meet strict eligibility criteria. Such loans will be secured against eligible mortgage/housing loans. Moving beyond conventional mortgage financing, KMRC will pilot innovative refinancing products tailored to underserved market segments. These offerings will include microloans, incremental construction lending, and green mortgages and are designed to address affordability, informality, and climate resilience gaps.Component 2: Support to land administration reforms to accelerate affordable housing. This component would support the State Department of Lands and Physical Planning (SDLPP) in its reforms to make property registration faster, cheaper and more reliable, which is a precondition to develop the mortgage market. Building off the technical assistance completed under KAFHP 1, the Project support will be structured into three sub-components: (2.1) enabling the environment for property and mortgage registration through legal and regulatory reforms: supporting the SDLPP to review, harmonize, and enact legislation to support end-to-end digital land transactions and promote consistency across land administration statutes; (2.2) digitization of spatial and land records data, including geodetic improvements: digitizing existing property rights data (titles, deeds, transfer documents, etc.) and graphical spatial data (survey plans, etc.) so that it is fully available in the digital system; and (2.3) enhancing the Ardhisasa land administration management system by addressing systemic issues: improving the system via business process reengineering to simplify processes, enhance automatic data checks, e-signature and other functionality.Component 3: Sustainable Financing Instruments. Subcomponent 3.1: Sovereign Sustainability-Linked Loan involves the development and issuance of a sovereign Sustainability Linked Loan (SLL) program, to leverage the GoK's strong sustainability agenda to diversify investors and access more affordable international capital. Sovereign borrowing in Kenya has been costly, making innovative financing tools such as this more attractive. Subcomponent 3.2: Sustainability Bond Issuance. Building on the robust framework and market precedent set by sovereign, KMRC is preparing for its inaugural sustainability bond issuance. This approach will enable KMRC to attract a diverse pool of investors and enhance market credibility. The transition to sustainability bonds will also support KMRC’s commitment to environmental and social impact, further strengthening its role in Kenya’s financial ecosystem. The World Bank financing will provide credit enhancements for the sovereign and sub-sovereign issuances.Component 4: Technical Assistance and Project Management: The TA will support Component 1 and overall project management. Activities will cover the development of new product offerings and assess market readiness. It will help define product characteristics and support the development and implementation of roll out plans. The TA will also help operationalize the Kenya Mortgage Guarantee Trust (KMGT), including capacity building and procurement of relevant IT systems. Additionally, the TA will support preparations for future bond issuances through transaction advisory services and consultancy support. This component will also finance project management and related capacity building.
